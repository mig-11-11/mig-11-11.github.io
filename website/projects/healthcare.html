PROJECT / UNDERGRADUATE RESEARCH

Adaptive Healthcare Referral Framework

An optimization and reward-learning framework for evaluating patient transfers across healthcare networks while balancing clinical compatibility, hospital-acquired infection risks, and patient privacy.

System Design Architecture Python Programming Network Modeling Operations Research Algorithm Optimization Data Preprocessing Pipelines Resource Allocation Heuristics Local Environment Isolation

Overview

Hospital-acquired infections (HAIs) are a major healthcare burden, with approximately 518,000 infections reported in U.S. acute care hospitals in 2023 alone. Patient transfers and inter-hospital referrals significantly influence infection exposure across healthcare facilities.

This research introduces a stochastic, risk-aware referral framework designed to model patient transfers through a Healthcare Referral Network (HRN). By expressing quality of care as reward values, the model optimizes referral decisions by balancing clinical compatibility, contagion potential, and patient privacy.

Objectives & Problem

Existing referral systems often fail to account for real-time dynamic contagion risk or privacy requirements during inter-hospital routing. Furthermore, observed referral reward data is highly sparse across large hospital networks.

Broad Goals

  • Model patient transfers through healthcare networks stochastically.
  • Incorporate infection risk (Contagion Potential) and patient privacy into clinical decision-making.
  • Optimize referral decisions by balancing clinical compatibility, infection risk, and privacy requirements.
  • Validate referral policy efficacy through regression and correlation analysis against reward values.

System Model & Data


The Healthcare Referral Network (HRN) is modeled as a directed graph $G = (V, E)$, where each node $u \in V$ represents a healthcare center and each directed arc $(u, v) \in E$ denotes allowable patient referrals from facility $u$ to $v$. Hospitals are evaluated using a dynamic Contagion Potential (CP) measure to gauge infection risk.

Due to missing transfer outcomes, a sparse Patient-Hospital Transfer Matrix captures observed health rewards following importance sampling to handle matrix sparsity. Referrals strictly adhere to the overlaying HRN, preventing transfers over non-existent network arcs.

Methodology & Optimization


The framework processes patient features and referral events using a structured three-stage optimization pipeline:

01 — Preprocessing & Tracking

A Patient Tracker $T = \{T_1, T_2, \dots, T_m\}$ monitors movements $(pat_i, source_i, dest_i)$ across $m$ transfers. Patient features $X$ and hospital attributes are preprocessed into baseline reward values. Importance sampling is applied to the Patient-Hospital Transfer Matrix to mitigate data sparsity and ensure robust reward estimation across unobserved referral paths.

02 — Contagion Risk Rewarding

Observed rewards for patient $i$ are formulated relative to destination safety: $$R_i = \frac{H_{sim}(T_{pat_i}, T_{dest_i})}{CP(dest_i)},$$ where $H_{sim}$ measures the Hamming similarity between patient features and hospital attributes, and $CP$ is the Contagion Potential of the destination hospital.


03 — Regularized Optimization

Learned weights $W$ to fit observed rewards using log-loss with entropy regularization parameter $\lambda$: $$\max_{W} \left(\underbrace{\frac{K}{|T|}\sum_{t \in T} R_t W_{feat_t,dest_t}}_{\text{reward / data-fitting term}}+\underbrace{\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{|X|}\sum_{k=1}^{K}W_{X_i,k}\log(W_{X_i,k}+\epsilon)}_{\text{entropy regularization}}\right)$$

Results & Parameter Sensitivity

The Python-based implementation was evaluated on an HRN dataset configured with $n=50$ patients, $K=7$ hospitals, and $m=100$ transfers over multiple iterations.

Scatter plots and regression trends demonstrate a strong positive correlation between empirical rewards and learned weight values across 100 iterations. Parameter sensitivity testing revealed that increasing the entropy regularization parameter $\lambda$ (from $0.2$ to $0.8$) preserves the positive trend while introducing controlled uncertainty in learned weights.

Conclusions & Ongoing Work

  • Correlation analyses confirm that optimized referrals meet patient clinical needs, minimize infection exposure, and uphold privacy guarantees.
  • Experimental results remain robust when scaling across varying numbers of hospitals and patients.
  • Demonstrates strong promise for deployment in real, evolving clinical environments and dynamic outbreak response settings.
  • Ongoing Work: Expanding validation to large-scale HRN datasets and adapting the framework for distinct disease groups.
