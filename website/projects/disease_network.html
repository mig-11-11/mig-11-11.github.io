PROJECT / UNDERGRADUATE RESEARCH

Disease Progression Network

A dynamic healthcare framework that infers directed disease progression networks from co-occurrence data and continuously adapts influence weights in near real-time using an incremental stochastic refinement algorithm, eliminating the need for costly full-model retraining.

Real-Time Streaming Algorithms Scalability Optimization Adaptive Modeling System Stability Mathematical Proofs Statistical Tracking Python Engineering

1. Research Question & Gap

Modern healthcare recommender systems rely on personalized clinical data, yet static machine learning models suffer from calibration drift as patient demographics, disease prevalence, and treatment protocols evolve over time. Existing solutions require frequent, computationally expensive model retraining from scratch (taking upwards of 3,100+ seconds) to maintain prediction accuracy.

Core Research Question: Can we construct directed disease progression networks and continuously adapt their transition influence weights in near real-time from streaming patient trajectories, preserving high rank and linear concordance with full-scale re-estimations without incurring the cost of full offline retraining?

2. Methodology & Preprocessing

Data & Preprocessing

Evaluations were performed on the MIMIC-III critical care dataset, comprising de-identified clinical records across intensive care unit admissions. Patient diagnoses were mapped to International Classification of Diseases, 9th Revision (ICD9) codes spanning 17 official disease superclasses. Degenerate features (disease columns with zero observed prevalence across all patients) were removed prior to matrix formulation.

Network Construction & Sparsification

The complete directed disease network $G(V, E)$ is initially inferred using the GENIE3 framework in Random Forest mode with 200 trees. For each disease node $u \in V$, its prevalence $x_u$ is modeled as a function of all other diseases $X_{-u}$. The feature importance scores serve as directed edge weights $w(u,v)$, quantifying the probabilistic influence of disease $u$ on disease $v$.

To eliminate noisy low-weight interactions, the graph was sparsified using the Kneedle algorithm. The maximum curvature point in the edge-weight distribution identified a sharp knee point cutoff at $w = 0.001538$, paring the initial 48.7M potential edges down to 680,370 active directed edges.

Figure 1. Identification of the knee point cutoff for edge sparsification (left) and depth-first extraction of high-influence subnetwork clusters (right).

Incremental Refinement & Adjustment Algorithm

Rather than retraining GENIE3 from scratch when new data arrives, the Adjustment Algorithm dynamically updates edge weights from streaming patient diffusion trajectories. In each iteration, a batch of $H$ patient trajectories is converted into an empirical edge-frequency subnetwork $H$, where $w_H(u,v)$ represents observed transition frequencies:

01 — Empirical Frequency Update

For observed edges $(u,v) \in H$, edge weights in $G$ are updated via: $$w_G(u,v) \leftarrow (1-\delta)w_G(u,v) + \delta \cdot w_H(u,v)$$ followed by projection onto $[0, 1]$.

02 — Mass Drift Tracking

Total edge weight drift is calculated across the graph to track global parameter shift: $$\Delta = \sum_{(u,v)\in G} w_{new}(u,v) - \sum_{(u,v)\in G} w_{old}(u,v)$$

03 — Conservation Redistribution

To prevent weight inflation, mass $\Delta$ is proportionally redistributed across unsupported edges $L = \{(u,v) \in G \mid f(u,v) = 0\}$: $$w_G(u,v) \leftarrow w_G(u,v) - \frac{w_G(u,v)}{\sum_{L} w_G(x,y)} \cdot \Delta$$

04 — Parameter Decay & Convergence

The integration rate decays exponentially ($\delta \leftarrow \gamma \cdot \delta$, default $\gamma = 0.9985$), ensuring geometric convergence of weight bias while bounding steady-state variance to $\frac{\delta}{2-\delta} \sigma_\eta^2$.

3. Analysis

Quasi-Patient Trajectory Simulation & Subtyping

To analyze temporal progression patterns, we simulated cohorts of quasi-patient trajectories using degree-weighted seed sampling and weighted random walks over the directed network. Pairwise trajectory distance matrices based on shared contiguous edge overlaps ($D_{ij} = \frac{1}{|E_i \cap E_j| + 1}$) were evaluated using Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering. Optimal cohort subtyping was identified at 1,899 distinct trajectory clusters via Silhouette score optimization.

Figure 2. Representative temporal disease progression pathways across discovered patient clusters.

Convergence & Empirical Tracking

The adjustment algorithm was evaluated across 5,000 iterative batches (batch size $H=50$ patients, sequence length 25) starting from a sparse initial graph. Network alignment against full ground-truth re-estimation was tracked using Kendall's rank correlation ($\tau$) and Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC).

Figure 3. Progression of Kendall's Tau ($\tau \approx 0.77$) and PCC ($\approx 0.67$) over 5,000 batches. along with batch runtime execution bounds.

Figure 4. Batch runtime execution bounds over 5,000 batches, demonstrating sub-second per-batch processing time (~1.08s) and 3,000x+ speedup versus full retraining (3,158s).

Clinical Progression Interpretation

Comparing inferred network modules against standard clinical taxonomies revealed a moderate pairwise concordance of 0.59, proving that data-driven networks capture multi-system progression dynamics missed by traditional ICD9 disease categories:

  • Cascading Failure Pathways: Ectopic pregnancy leading to severe pulmonary insufficiency and respiratory failure (Cluster 4: $6332 \rightarrow 5152 \rightarrow 4858$).
  • Cross-System Dysfunctions: Valvular heart disease coupling with chronic obstructive lung disease and pulmonary collapse (Cluster 26: $3979 \rightarrow 4995 \rightarrow 4994$).
  • Post-Insult Vulnerabilities: Neurological stroke insult leading to secondary aspiration and upper respiratory infections (Cluster 209: $4340 \rightarrow 4690 \rightarrow 4705$).
  • Chronic Relapsing Loops: Bidirectional cyclic transitions in functional bowel disorders (Cluster 1698: $5647 \leftrightarrow 5648 \leftrightarrow 5651$).

4. Results & Takeaways


The incremental stochastic refinement approach demonstrated near-equivalent structural fidelity to full offline retraining while delivering massive computational speedups suitable for real-time streaming clinical workflows.

Key Takeaways & Contributions

  • Sub-Second Adaptation: Formulated an incremental refinement algorithm operating in $O(H \cdot |V|)$ linear per-iteration complexity, reducing updates to ~1.08 seconds per batch.
  • Mathematical Guarantees: Derived theoretical convergence bounds proving geometric bias decay ($\epsilon_{t+1} = (1-\delta)^t \epsilon_0$) and bounded steady-state update variance ($\frac{\delta}{2-\delta} \sigma_\eta^2$).
  • Mass Conservation: Prevented calibration drift under concept shift via mass-conserving weight redistribution over unobserved edges.

Future Extensions

Future work includes incorporating diagnosis-frequency-aware weighting schemes to distinguish repetitive chronic conditions from acute events, establishing automated drift-detection modules to trigger edge unlearning, and expanding the pipeline to privacy-preserving federated multi-center deployments.
