PROJECT / INDEPENDENT

NFL Performance Modeling & Analytics

A statistical analysis of NFL team and player performance using play-by-play data from the 2020–2025 regular seasons. The project examines which NFL statistics are associated with team success.

R Programming Data Engineering & ETL Statistical Modeling Data Visualization Quantitative Analysis Database Architecture Automated Reporting Sports Analytics

01 / OVERVIEW

Connecting performance to winning

NFL teams generate hundreds of measurable performance statistics throughout a season, but not all statistics capture the same information about team success. This project investigates which offensive, defensive, player-level, and situational statistics are most strongly associated with winning.

The analysis focuses on whether efficiency-based measures such as Expected Points Added ($\text{EPA}$) provide additional information beyond traditional statistics, while also examining red-zone performance, explosive plays, quarterback efficiency, third-down conversion, and early-game performance.

02 / DATA

Data & Preparation

Primary Dataset

The primary dataset consists of NFL play-by-play observations from the 2020–2025 regular seasons ($N = 3{,}230$ team-games across $n = 192$ team-seasons). Each observation contains information about game state, teams, play type, yardage, expected points added ($\text{EPA}$), success, turnovers, and situational variables.

Pipeline & Tooling

The raw play-level data was collected using the nflreadr and tidyverse packages in R and transformed into analysis-ready datasets using SQL. Statistical analysis was performed in Python using NumPy, Pandas, Matplotlib, SciPy, and Statsmodels.

03 / METHODOLOGY

Statistical Modeling Pipeline

01 — Data Processing

Raw play-by-play observations were aggregated with SQL into game-level and team-season datasets containing wins, win percentage ($\text{Win}\%$), efficiency measures, and situational statistics.

02 — Feature Engineering

Play-level data was transformed into metrics including $\text{EPA}/\text{play}$, success rate, turnover rate, red-zone efficiency, explosive-play rate, quarterback efficiency, and third-down performance.

03 — Statistical Analysis

Pearson ($r$) and Spearman ($\rho$) correlations were used to measure associations, while ordinary least squares (OLS) regression modeled team win percentage and logistic regression modeled individual game outcomes: $$\text{logit}(P(Y=1)) = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \beta_i X_i$$

04 — Model Evaluation

Models were evaluated using coefficient of determination ($R^2$), adjusted $R^2_{\text{adj}}$, coefficient significance ($p < 0.05$), confidence intervals, $F$-tests, likelihood-ratio tests, and comparisons between alternative statistical specifications.

04 / RESULTS

What did the models show?

$\text{EPA}$ consistently captured information about team success

$\text{EPA}/\text{play}$ showed a strong association with team win percentage across multiple analyses. The $\text{EPA}$-based offensive model explained $54.3\%$ of observed variation in win percentage ($R^2 = 0.543$), compared with $R^2 = 0.516$ for the traditional-statistics model.


The final efficiency model incorporated offensive $\text{EPA}/\text{play}$, offensive success rate, offensive turnover rate, defensive $\text{EPA}/\text{play}$, and defensive success rate. Together, these variables explained $77.8\%$ of the observed variation ($R^2 = 0.778$) in team win percentage within the analyzed sample.

The results also showed that red-zone $\text{EPA}/\text{play}$, quarterback $\text{EPA}/\text{play}$, and third-down conversion rate were each substantially associated with team success. These relationships remained visible across both correlation and regression-based analyses.

05 / KEY FINDINGS

Eight Analytical Questions

  • Red-zone efficiency: Red-zone $\text{EPA}/\text{play}$ had the strongest pairwise association with team win percentage among the red-zone measures examined ($r = 0.512$, $p < 0.001$).
  • $\text{EPA}$ versus traditional statistics: $\text{EPA}/\text{play}$ had a stronger individual correlation with win percentage than yards per play and success rate, with $r = 0.734$.
  • Explosive plays: Explosive-play rate was associated with winning independently, but was not statistically significant ($p > 0.05$) after overall $\text{EPA}$ efficiency was included in the model.
  • Player efficiency: Workload thresholds were used to identify high-efficiency quarterback, running back, and receiver seasons while reducing small-sample instability.
  • Quarterback performance: $\text{QB EPA}/\text{play}$ had the strongest pairwise association with team win percentage among $\text{QB EPA}/\text{play}$, $\text{CPOE}$ (Completion Percentage Above Expectation), and $\text{QB}$ success rate.
  • Third down: Third-down conversion rate had a Pearson correlation of $r = 0.640$ with team win percentage.
  • Early-game performance: Teams in the highest first-half $\text{EPA}$ quartile won $74.3\%$ of games, compared with $24.1\%$ for teams in the lowest quartile.
  • Underlying efficiency: A multivariate efficiency model explained $77.8\%$ of observed variation ($R^2 = 0.778$) in team win percentage within the sample.

07 / LIMITATIONS

Interpreting the results

The analysis is observational and measures associations within the 2020–2025 sample. Statistical significance ($p < 0.05$) does not establish that changing a performance statistic would cause a corresponding change in win percentage.

The reported $R^2$ values also describe in-sample explanatory power, rather than validated future-season prediction. The fitted values ($\hat{y}$) from the final efficiency model therefore identify in-sample deviations from the modeled relationship rather than independently validated forecasts.

Other factors—including opponent quality, injuries, coaching, schedule strength, home-field advantage, special teams, and game state—are not fully represented in the current models.

08 / FUTURE WORK

From explanation to forecasting

The next stage of the project is to evaluate whether the observed relationships generalize to future seasons through chronological out-of-sample validation. One proposed approach is to train on 2020–2024 and evaluate on 2025, followed by rolling-origin validation across multiple season boundaries.

Future models could incorporate opponent-adjusted efficiency, recent performance, home-field advantage, injuries, and other contextual variables. Logistic regression could provide an interpretable baseline before comparing more flexible machine-learning and ensemble methods.

09 / CONCLUSION

A foundation for predictive modeling

Across the eight analyses, efficiency-based statistics—particularly $\text{EPA}/\text{play}$—contained substantial information about NFL team success. The consistency of the relationship across red-zone, offensive, quarterback, and full-team analyses supports the use of expected-points measures as a framework for summarizing performance.

At the same time, the analysis demonstrates why statistical modeling requires more than a single correlation ($r$) or high $R^2$. Predictor overlap, observational confounding, sample structure, and the distinction between in-sample explanation and out-of-sample prediction all affect how the results should be interpreted.

The project therefore provides a foundation for extending the analysis toward a temporally validated NFL game-outcome forecasting framework.
